1. Rules Based on Age & Weight

1
Lecture Question · Slide 4
Drug dosage based on age — write the rule:
a. Young's rule

Dose for child = Age / (Age + 12) × Adult dose

Age in years.

For children (years).

b. Cowling's rule

Dose for child = Age at next birthday × Adult dose / 24

Uses the age at the next birthday.

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

c. Fried's rule for infants

Dose for infant = Age (in months) × Adult dose / 150

Age in months.

Infants only.

Extra Question · Young's rule
Using Young's rule, calculate the dose for a 6-year-old child if the adult dose is 250 mg.
Child dose = ?

83.3 mg

Age / (Age + 12) × Adult dose
= 6 / (6 + 12) × 250 = 6/18 × 250 = 83.3 mg

Young's rule uses the age in years.

Extra Question · Young's rule
Using Young's rule, calculate the dose for an 8-year-old child if the adult dose is 500 mg.
Child dose = ?

200 mg

Age / (Age + 12) × Adult dose
= 8 / (8 + 12) × 500 = 8/20 × 500 = 200 mg

Young's rule uses the age in years.

Extra Question · Cowling's rule
Using Cowling's rule, calculate the dose for a child who is 5 years old (age at next birthday = 6) if the adult dose is 300 mg.
Child dose = ?

75 mg

Age at next birthday × Adult dose / 24
= 6 × 300 / 24 = 75 mg

Use the age at the NEXT birthday.

Extra Question · Cowling's rule

Use the age at the NEXT birthday.

Extra Question · Fried's rule
Using Fried's rule, calculate the dose for a 6-month-old infant if the adult dose is 250 mg.

10 mg

Age (months) × Adult dose / 150
= 6 × 250 / 150 = 10 mg

Fried's rule: age in months ÷ 150.

Lecture Question · Slide 5
Drug dosage based on weight — write Clark's rule.
Clark's rule

Dose for child = Weight (lb) × Adult dose / 150

150 lb = average adult weight.

Weight must be in lb (kg × 2.2).

Extra Question · Clark's rule
Using Clark's rule, calculate the dose for a child weighing 45 lb if the adult dose is 500 mg.
Child dose = ?

150 mg

Weight (lb) × Adult dose / 150
= 45 × 500 / 150 = 150 mg

Weight must be in lb.

Extra Question · Clark's rule
Using Clark's rule, calculate the dose for a child weighing 20 kg if the adult dose is 300 mg. (1 kg = 2.2 lb)
Child dose = ?

88 mg

20 kg × 2.2 = 44 lb
44 × 300 / 150 = 88 mg

Convert kg → lb first.

Textbook Question · Worked example · Page 132
BNF: "for most drugs the adult maximum dose should not be exceeded". A drug has a dose of 8 mg/kg and a maximum dose of 300 mg.
Dose for a 10-kg child and a 40-kg child = ?

80 mg and 300 mg

10 × 8 = 80 mg
40 × 8 = 320 mg → exceeds the maximum → give 300 mg

Never go above the adult maximum.

Extra Question · Adult maximum dose
A drug has a dose of 10 mg/kg and an adult maximum dose of 400 mg. Calculate the dose for a 15-kg child and a 45-kg child.
Doses = ?

150 mg and 400 mg

15 × 10 = 150 mg ✓
45 × 10 = 450 mg → exceeds 400 mg → give 400 mg

Never exceed the adult maximum.

Extra Question · Adult maximum dose

Doses = ?

240 mg and 500 mg

12 × 20 = 240 mg ✓
30 × 20 = 600 mg → exceeds 500 mg → give 500 mg

Never exceed the adult maximum.

Lecture Question · Slide 16
A pediatric patient receives enalaprilat IV every 12 hours: 5 mcg/kg → 55 mcg per dose. Convert to oral enalapril 100 mcg/kg as a single daily dose. A 2.5-mg tablet is crushed and made up to 12.5 mL with sterile water. Dose in mL?
Volume = ?

5.5 mL

Weight: 55 / 5 = 11 kg
Oral dose: 100 × 11 = 1100 mcg = 1.1 mg
Concentration: 2.5 mg / 12.5 mL = 0.2 mg/mL
1.1 / 0.2 = 5.5 mL

Get the weight from the IV dose first. No answer on the slide — solved by us.

Extra Question · Find weight from a dose, then convert
A child receives 120 mcg of a drug per IV dose, based on 10 mcg/kg. The doctor switches to an oral dose of 50 mcg/kg once daily. A 5-mg tablet is crushed and made up to 25 mL with water. Calculate the oral dose in mL.
Volume = ?

3 mL

Weight = 120 / 10 = 12 kg
Oral dose = 50 × 12 = 600 mcg = 0.6 mg
Concentration = 5 / 25 = 0.2 mg/mL
0.6 / 0.2 = 3 mL

Weight first, then mcg → mg, then mL.

Extra Question · Find weight from a dose, then convert

Volume = ?

6 mL

Weight = 30 / 2 = 15 kg
Oral dose = 80 × 15 = 1200 mcg = 1.2 mg
Concentration = 2 / 10 = 0.2 mg/mL
1.2 / 0.2 = 6 mL

Weight first, then mcg → mg, then mL.

Fluconazole for mucosal candidiasis in children is 3 mg/kg daily. Calculate the dose for a child aged 3 years and a child aged 11 years and suggest a formulation (capsules 50, 150, 200 mg; suspension 50 mg/5 mL, 200 mg/5 mL).

42 mg = 4.2 mL of 50 mg/5 mL

Table 8.1: 14 kg → 3 × 14 = 42 mg
42 × 5 / 50 = 4.2 mL daily

Under 5 → liquid is better.

b. 11-year-old

≈ 105 mg (≈ 100 mg): two 50-mg capsules or 10 mL of 50 mg/5 mL

3 × 35 = 105 mg

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

Lecture Question · Slide 6
Drug dosage on body surface area: BSA = √(Ht (cm) × Wt (kg) / 3600) (or use nomogram).
a. Child dose from the adult dose = ?

BSA of child / 1.73 m² × Adult dose

1.73 m² = average adult BSA.

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

b. Child dose if the dose per m² is given = ?

BSA of child × Dose per m²

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

Lecture Question · Slide 8
The usual pediatric dose of ephedrine sulfate is 25 mg/m². Calculate the dose for a child weighing 18 kg and measuring 82 cm in height.
Dose = ?

16 mg

25 × 0.64 = 16 mg

Dose per m² given → BSA × dose. No answer on the slide — solved by us.
