1. Rules Based on Age & Weight

1
Lecture Question · Slide 4
Drug dosage based on age — write the rule:
a. Young's rule

Dose for child = Age / (Age + 12) × Adult dose

Age in years.

For children (years).

b. Cowling's rule

Dose for child = Age at next birthday × Adult dose / 24

Uses the age at the next birthday.

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

c. Fried's rule for infants

Dose for infant = Age (in months) × Adult dose / 150

Age in months.

Infants only.

2
Lecture Question · Slide 5
Drug dosage based on weight — write Clark's rule.
Clark's rule

Dose for child = Weight (lb) × Adult dose / 150

150 lb = average adult weight.

Weight must be in lb (kg × 2.2).

Textbook Question · Worked example · Page 132
BNF: "for most drugs the adult maximum dose should not be exceeded". A drug has a dose of 8 mg/kg and a maximum dose of 300 mg.
Dose for a 10-kg child and a 40-kg child = ?

80 mg and 300 mg

10 × 8 = 80 mg
40 × 8 = 320 mg → exceeds the maximum → give 300 mg

Never go above the adult maximum.

3. Doses Based on Body Surface Area (BSA)

Lecture Question · Slide 6
Drug dosage on body surface area: BSA = √(Ht (cm) × Wt (kg) / 3600) (or use nomogram).
a. Child dose from the adult dose = ?

BSA of child / 1.73 m² × Adult dose

1.73 m² = average adult BSA.

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

b. Child dose if the dose per m² is given = ?

BSA of child × Dose per m²

Weight (kg) or BSA × dose per kg / per m² → then convert to volume.

17
Lecture Question · Slide 8
The usual pediatric dose of ephedrine sulfate is 25 mg/m². Calculate the dose for a child weighing 18 kg and measuring 82 cm in height.
Dose = ?

16 mg

BSA = √(82 × 18 / 3600) = √0.41 = 0.64 m²
25 × 0.64 = 16 mg

Dose per m² given → BSA × dose. No answer on the slide — solved by us.
⚠️ The slide writes "25/m²" — taken as 25 mg/m².
