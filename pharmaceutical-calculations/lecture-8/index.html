1. Giving Sets & Drip Rate (drops/min)

2 lecture · 4 textbook (total 6)

1
Lecture Question · Slide 4
Giving sets — how many drops are in 1 mL?
a. Standard giving set (SGS)

20 drops/mL for crystalloids · 15 drops/mL for colloids

Crystalloids: e.g. sodium chloride, dextrose.
Colloids: e.g. blood.

Use 20 drops/mL unless the question says otherwise.

b. Microdrop giving set (burette)

60 drops/mL

Microdrop = small drops → more drops per mL.

2
Lecture Question · Slide 5
One litre of 5% dextrose is to be given over 8 hours. What drip rate is required? (rate: 20 drops/mL)
Drip rate = ?

42 drops/min

Step 1: 1000 mL × 20 = 20 000 drops
Step 2: 8 h × 60 = 480 min
Step 3: 20 000 / 480 = 41.67 → round up → 42 drops/min

Drops can only be whole → round to a whole number.

Textbook Question · Example 11.3 · Page 197
20 mL of Addiphos solution and 10 mL of multivitamin infusion are added to 500 mL of glucose 5% solution. The resultant solution is to be administered over 4 hours. The administration set is calibrated to 20 drops/mL. Calculate the number of drops per minute to be given if the patient is to receive all of the solution in the specified time.
Drops/min = ?

44 drops/min

Total volume = 20 + 10 + 500 = 530 mL
530 × 20 = 10 600 drops
4 × 60 = 240 min
10 600 / 240 = 44.17 → 44 drops/min

When solutions are mixed, use the TOTAL volume.

2. Converting mL/hour, mL/min & drops/min

1 lecture · 2 textbook (total 3)

7
Lecture Question · Slide 6
One litre of sodium chloride 0.9% has to be given at a rate of 120 mL/hour. What is the rate in drops/min? (20 drops/mL)
Rate = ? drops/min

40 drops/min

Step 1: 120 mL/hour = 120 mL / 60 min
Step 2: 120 / 60 = 2 mL/min
Step 3: 2 mL × 20 drops = 40 drops/min

mL/hour ÷ 60 = mL/min; × 20 = drops/min. The 1 litre is not needed.

3.33 mL/min

5 h = 300 min
1000 / 300 = 3.33 mL/min

mL ÷ minutes.

3. Dose Rates (mcg/kg/min) & mL/hour

2 lecture (total 2)

10
Lecture Question · Slide 7
You have an infusion of dopamine 800 mg in 500 mL. The dose required is 2 mcg/kg/min for a patient weighing 68 kg. (20 drops/mL)
a. What is the rate in mL/hour?

≈ 5.1 mL/hour

2 × 68 = 136 mcg/min
136 × 60 = 8160 mcg/hour = 8.16 mg/hour
800 mg → 500 mL
8.16 mg → x
x = 8.16 × 500 / 800 = 5.1 mL/hour (slide: ≈ 5 mL/hour)

Dose/kg/min × weight × 60 → mg/hour, then convert mg → mL.

b. What is the rate in drops/min?

≈ 1.7 drops/min

5.1 mL/hour × 20 = 102 drops/hour
102 / 60 = 1.7 drops/min (slide: 100 / 60 = 1.7)

mL/hour × 20 → drops/hour, ÷ 60 → drops/min.

11
Lecture Question · Slide 8
An infusion pump containing 250 mg dobutamine in 50 mL is administered at a rate of 3.5 mL/hour. Convert to mcg/kg/min. (Patient weight: 70 kg)
Dose = ? mcg/kg/min

≈ 4.2 mcg/kg/min

250 mg → 50 mL
x → 3.5 mL → x = 17.5 mg/hour
17.5 / 60 = 0.2917 mg/min = 291.7 mcg/min
291.7 / 70 = 4.17 ≈ 4.2 mcg/kg/min

The reverse of slide 7: mL/hour → mg/hour → mcg/min → ÷ weight.

4. Volume, Drip Rate & Duration

12
Lecture Question · Slide 9
A prescription of one litre 5% dextrose to be administered over 8 hours at a drip rate of 40 drops/min. You wish to check the drip rate. How long will it take for the infusion to finish? (20 drops/mL)
Time = ?

500 min = 8 h 20 min

1000 mL × 20 = 20 000 drops
20 000 / 40 = 500 min
500 / 60 = 8.33 hours = 8 h 20 min

Total drops ÷ drops per minute = minutes.
⚠️ The slide writes "8 hours, 18 mins" (0.3 × 60). Exactly: 0.333 h × 60 = 20 min → 8 h 20 min. It also means 40 drops/min is a little too slow for 8 hours.

Textbook Question · Example 11.2 · Page 196
The recommended flow rate for drug Y is 2–3 mL/minute at a concentration of 5 mg/mL. Drug Y is supplied in 500-mg vials for dilution in glucose 5%. Using one vial, the flow rate is 3 mL/minute and the IV set is calibrated to 20 drops/mL.
a. Volume of glucose 5% solution to use?

100 mL

5 mg → 1 mL
500 mg → y → y = 100 mL

Make the whole vial up to 5 mg/mL.

b. Drops per minute?

60 drops/min

3 mL/min × 20 = 60 drops/min

Volume → drops (× drop factor), time → minutes, then divide.

c. How long will the infusion take?

33.3 min

100 mL / 3 mL per min = 33.3 min

Volume ÷ rate = time.

Textbook Question · Q5 · Page 209
Nizatidine injection (25 mg/mL) is supplied in 4-mL vials. The BNF states that for continuous infusion "dilute 300 mg in 150 mL and give at a rate of 10 mg/hour". If this procedure is adopted, calculate:
a. How many vials are required?

3 vials

One vial = 25 × 4 = 100 mg
300 / 100 = 3 vials

Volume → drops (× drop factor), time → minutes, then divide.

b. The flow rate (mL/hour)?

5 mL/hour

Concentration = 300 mg / 150 mL = 2 mg/mL
10 mg/hour ÷ 2 mg/mL = 5 mL/hour

Volume → drops (× drop factor), time → minutes, then divide.

c. The length of time the infusion will take?

30 hours

150 mL / 5 mL per hour = 30 h (or 300 mg / 10 mg per hour)

Volume → drops (× drop factor), time → minutes, then divide.

7. Syringe Drivers (mm/hour)

26
Textbook Question · Example 11.8 · Page 204
Apomorphine injection (10 mg/mL) is diluted with an equal volume of sodium chloride 0.9% and put in a 10-mL syringe that is 60 mm long. The patient requires 2.5 mg of apomorphine per hour. At what rate (mm/hour) should the syringe driver be set?
Rate = ? mm/hour

3 mm/hour

5 mL injection + 5 mL NaCl → 50 mg in 10 mL (60 mm)
50 mg → 60 mm
2.5 mg → x = 3 mm
So 3 mm/hour

Syringe length ↔ amount of drug.

Vials and diluent = ?

4 vials · 16 mL diluent

100 / 5 = 20 mg/hour → 2 mL must contain 20 mg
20 mL → 200 mg
200 / 50 = 4 vials (4 mL)
Diluent = 20 − 4 = 16 mL

Volume → drops (× drop factor), time → minutes, then divide.
