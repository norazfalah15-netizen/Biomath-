1. Density

1 lecture · 1 textbook (total 2)

1
Lecture Question · Slides 3–4
Density — the basics.
a. 1 mL of water at 20 °C weighs?

1 g (1000 mL = 1000 g = 1 kg)

Slide 3: this 1 : 1 weight/volume relationship is not true for other liquids, solids and semisolids.

Only water has 1 mL = 1 g.

b. Density of a liquid = ?

Weight per unit volume (g/mL)

Slide 4: other references express density as weight/mL.

Density = g ÷ mL.

c. A very mobile liquid is easier to measure by ___, and a viscous liquid is easier to measure by ___

Volume · weight

Slide 4: very mobile liquid → easier to express as volume; viscous liquid → easier to convert volume to weight.

Runny → measure in mL; thick → weigh it.

d. Specific gravity = ?

Density of a substance ÷ density of a reference substance

Slide 4: sometimes the specific gravity is given instead of the density.

The reference is usually water (1 g/mL), so sp. gr. has the same number as the density.

2
Textbook Question · Q5 · Page 159
If the weight of 40 mL of a liquid is 37.5 g, what is the density of the liquid in g/mL?
Density = ? g/mL

0.938 g/mL

37.5 / 40 = 0.9375 ≈ 0.938 g/mL

Density = weight ÷ volume.

2. Converting Volume ↔ Weight

1 lecture · 6 textbook (total 7)

3
Lecture Question · Slide 5
Convert 45 mL of liquid X to its equivalent weight. Liquid X has a weight per mL of 0.83 g.
Weight = ? g

37.35 g

1 mL → 0.83 g
45 mL → x
x = 45 × 0.83 = 37.35 g

Volume → weight: multiply by the density.

3. Liquids in Ointments

1 lecture · 2 textbook (total 3)

10
Lecture Question · Slide 6
You need to prepare 50 g of ointment using the following formula:
Zinc oxide 15 g
Liquid paraffin 20 mL
Yellow soft paraffin to 150 g
(Liquid paraffin density: 0.88 g/mL)
a. Liquid paraffin in the original formula = ? g

17.6 g

20 × 0.88 = 17.6 g

Change the mL to g first, so everything is in grams.

b. Yellow soft paraffin in the original formula = ? g

117.4 g

150 − (15 + 17.6) = 117.4 g

“To 150 g” = the total is 150 g.

c. Quantities for 50 g = ?

Zinc oxide 5 g · liquid paraffin 5.87 g · yellow soft paraffin 39.13 g

50 / 150 = ⅓ → divide everything by 3
15 / 3 = 5 g
17.6 / 3 = 5.87 g
117.4 / 3 = 39.13 g
Check: 5 + 5.87 + 39.13 = 50 g

Note: the handwriting on the slide writes 117.6 g and total = 180 g; the correct values are 117.4 g and a total of 150 g. Divided by 3 → 5.87 g and 39.13 g (the slide rounds to 5.8 g and 39.2 g).

A pharmacist has to prepare 25 g of an ointment to the following formula:
Zinc oxide 10 g
Liquid paraffin 15 mL
Yellow soft paraffin to 100 g
Calculate the final formula for 25 g of the ointment. (Density of liquid paraffin = 0.88 g/mL)
Quantities for 25 g = ?

Zinc oxide 2.5 g · liquid paraffin 3.3 g · yellow soft paraffin 19.2 g

Liquid paraffin: 15 × 0.88 = 13.2 g
25 / 100 = ¼
Zinc oxide: 10 / 4 = 2.5 g
Liquid paraffin: 13.2 / 4 = 3.3 g
Yellow soft paraffin: 25 − (2.5 + 3.3) = 19.2 g

Same steps as the lecture.

4. Displacement Volumes

2 lecture · 4 textbook (total 6)

13
Lecture Question · Slides 8–9
Displacement volumes — the basics.
a. Definition

The quantity of solvent that will be displaced by a specified quantity of solid during dissolution

Slide 8. Knowledge of displacement volumes is needed to calculate the volume of solvent to add to a known amount of solid to obtain an exact volume of solution.

The space the dissolved powder takes up.

b. 5 g salt + 95 mL water = ?

Above 95 mL but not exactly 100 mL (it may be less or more)

Slide 8: the 5 g salt dissolves and increases the total volume (it occupies a space and displaces some of the water).

Unlike 5 mL water + 95 mL water = 100 mL exactly.

c. Displacement volume of diamorphine = 0.06 mL/5 mg. Meaning?

Every 5 mg of drug displaces 0.06 mL of water

Slide 9: used when water for injection is added to lyophilised powder to make a known volume and concentration.

Read “0.06 mL per 5 mg”.

14
Lecture Question · Slide 10
Calculate the volume of water for injections required to produce 1 mL of injection containing 5 mg diamorphine. A vial of 5 mg diamorphine is available. Displacement volume is 0.06 mL/5 mg.
Water = ? mL

0.94 mL

Total volume = 1 mL
5 mg displaces 0.06 mL
1 − 0.06 = 0.94 mL of water

Water = final volume − displacement volume.

15
Textbook Question · Example 9.4 · Page 146
Same diamorphine injection (5 mg powder, displacement volume 0.06 mL/5 mg, 1 mL required). What would happen if the pharmacist ignored the displacement volume and added 1 mL of water to the powder?
a. Total volume = ? mL

1.06 mL

1 + 0.06 = 1.06 mL

The powder adds its own 0.06 mL.

b. Dose in 1 mL = ? mg

4.7 mg (instead of 5 mg)

5 mg in 1.06 mL
5 / 1.06 = 4.7 mg in 1 mL

The solution is more dilute → the patient gets a lower dose.

16
Textbook Question · Example 9.7 · Page 148
Diamorphine is available as a powder for reconstitution in quantities of 30 mg, 100 mg and 500 mg. Using the displacement volume of 0.06 mL/5 mg, calculate how much diluent will be displaced by each.
a. 30 mg = ? mL

0.36 mL

30 / 5 = 6 → 6 × 0.06 = 0.36 mL

Scale the 0.06 mL/5 mg.

b. 100 mg = ? mL

1.2 mL

100 / 5 = 20 → 20 × 0.06 = 1.2 mL

Same proportion.

c. 500 mg = ? mL

6.0 mL

500 / 5 = 100 → 100 × 0.06 = 6.0 mL

Same proportion.

Drug Z has a negligible displacement volume in water. If 10 mL of water is added to 50 mg of drug Z, what will be the final volume of the solution?
Final volume = ? mL

10 mL

Negligible displacement volume → the drug adds no volume

Negligible = ignore it.

Textbook Question · Q8 · Page 159
A pharmacist adds 75 mL of water to 35 g of a soluble drug powder and the volume of the final solution is 115 mL. Calculate the displacement volume of the drug powder.
Displacement volume = ?

1.14 mL/g

115 − 75 = 40 mL displaced by 35 g
40 / 35 = 1.14 mL/g

Final volume − water added = volume of the powder.

5. Diluent for a Required Concentration

1 lecture · 5 textbook (total 6)

19
Lecture Question · Slide 11
The displacement volume for drug X is 0.5 mL/50 mg. The drug is required at a strength of 2 mg/mL. Calculate the volume of diluent to be added to 50 mg of drug X.
a. Total volume = ? mL

25 mL

2 mg → 1 mL
50 mg → x
x = 50 / 2 = 25 mL

Total volume from the required concentration.

b. Diluent = ? mL

24.5 mL

25 − 0.5 = 24.5 mL

Total − displacement volume.

c. What if 40 mg of drug X were needed (same strength)? Diluent = ? mL

19.4 mL

Displacement: 50 mg → 0.5 mL, 40 mg → 0.4 mL
Total: 40 / 2 = 20 mL
20 − 0.4 = 19.4 mL

Scale the displacement volume to the new amount.

Textbook Question · Example 9.5 · Page 147
A 4-mL injection containing drug X 250 mg/mL is required for a patient. The displacement volume for drug X is 0.56 mL/g. What volume of water for injections should be added to the correct weight of drug X to produce 4 mL of the injection?
a. Drug X needed = ? g

1 g

250 × 4 = 1000 mg = 1 g

Concentration × volume.

b. Water = ? mL

3.44 mL

1 g displaces 0.56 mL
4 − 0.56 = 3.44 mL

Total − displacement volume.

21
Textbook Question · Q9 · Page 159
Ampicillin powder for reconstitution into an injection has a displacement volume of 0.8 mL/g. If 10 mL of water is added to 500 mg of the powder, calculate the total volume of the solution. What would be the concentration expressed as mg/mL?
a. Total volume = ? mL

10.4 mL

500 mg = 0.5 g → 0.5 × 0.8 = 0.4 mL
10 + 0.4 = 10.4 mL

Here we add: water + displacement = total.

b. Concentration = ? mg/mL

48.1 mg/mL

500 / 10.4 = 48.08 ≈ 48.1 mg/mL

Drug ÷ total volume.

Textbook Question · Q11 · Page 159
Drug X has a displacement volume of 0.1 mL/30 units and is required as an injection containing 6 units in 1 mL. Drug X is available as a powder in a vial containing 90 units. What volume of diluent should be added to the powder to produce the injection solution?
Diluent = ? mL

6. Antibiotic Mixtures

2 lecture (total 2)

25
Lecture Question · Slide 12
Displacement volumes for other formulations — why are antibiotic mixtures supplied as powders?
Explain

Many antibiotics hydrolyse when mixed with water, so they are supplied as a powder and mixed with an exact volume of water at the time of dispensing

Slide 12: small children cannot take tablets/capsules; injections are only for special cases; mixtures are preferred for children but have a short life span before hydrolysis. The exact volume of water depends on the manufacturer’s displacement volume.

Powder → add water when dispensing → short shelf life after mixing.

26
Lecture Question · Slide 14
You need to add 68 mL water to produce 100 mL of a mixture containing 250 mg/5 mL of drug X. Calculate the displacement volume for a quantity of powder equivalent to 250 mg of drug X.
a. Total drug in 100 mL = ? mg

5000 mg

250 mg → 5 mL
x → 100 mL
x = 5000 mg

Concentration × volume.

b. Volume displaced by the whole powder = ? mL

32 mL

100 − 68 = 32 mL

Final volume − water added.

c. Displacement volume for 250 mg = ? mL

1.6 mL

5000 mg → 32 mL
250 mg → x
x = 32 × 250 / 5000 = 1.6 mL

Or: 100 / 5 = 20 doses → 32 / 20 = 1.6 mL per dose. (Same as textbook Example 9.8, amoxicillin.)
