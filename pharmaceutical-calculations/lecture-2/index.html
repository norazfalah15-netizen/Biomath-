Lecture Question · Slide 5
Fractions & percentages: to convert a fraction to a percentage, multiply by 100; to convert a percentage to a fraction, divide by 100.
a. Convert 2/8 to a percentage.

25%

2/8 = 1/4
1/4 × 100 = 25%

Fraction → percentage: × 100.

b. Convert 25% to a fraction.

1/4

25% = 25/100 = 5/20 = 1/4 (= 2/8)

2. Calculations with %

3 lecture questions (total 3)

3
Lecture Question · Slide 7
How much is 35% in 500 ml?
Amount = ?

175 ml

Assume 500 = 100%
500 → 100
x → 35
x = 500 × 35 / 100 = 175 ml
OR use the formula: Number / 100 × % required = 500 / 100 × 35 = 175 ml

The whole amount is always 100%.

4
Lecture Question · Slide 8
What is the percentage of 480 of 8000?
Percentage = ?

6%

Assume 8000 is 100%
8000 → 100
480 → x
x = 480 × 100 / 8000 = 6%
OR use the formula: smaller number / larger number × 100 = 480 / 8000 × 100 = 6%

Part ÷ whole × 100.

5
Lecture Question · Slide 9
How many ml is 40% of 1.3 litre?
Volume (ml) = ?

520 ml

a) Convert litre to ml (because the required answer is in ml): 1.3 litre = 1.3 × 1000 = 1300 ml
b) Consider 1300 ml as 100%
c) Cross multiply: 1300 → 100, x → 40
x = 1300 × 40 / 100 = 520 ml

Change to the units the question asks for first.

Lecture Question · Slide 10
Ratio strength is expressed as a ratio in the form of 1 in x.
5 g of product contains 250 mg of sulphur in yellow soft paraffin. Express this in ratio.

1 in 20 (w/w)

This is w/w — must be converted to the same units first:
250 mg = 0.25 g
0.25 → 5
1 → x
x = 5 × 1 / 0.25 = 20
Ratio strength = 1 in 20

Same units for ingredient and product, then make the ingredient = 1.

4. Percentage Concentration (w/v, w/w, v/v)

14
Lecture Question · Slide 11
The most common way of describing drug concentration is percentage concentration.
a. What does 3% w/v mean?

3 g in 100 ml

w/v = weight in volume → 3% w/v means 3 g in 100 ml

Solid in liquid: grams per 100 ml.

b. What does 3% w/w mean?

3 g in 100 g

w/w = weight in weight → 3% w/w means 3 g in 100 g

Solid in solid: grams per 100 g.

c. What does 3% v/v mean?

3 ml in 100 ml

v/v = volume in volume → 3% v/v means 3 ml in 100 ml

Liquid in liquid: ml per 100 ml.

15
Lecture Question · Slide 12
How much dextrose is there in a 500 ml infusion of dextrose 4% w/v?
Dextrose = ?

20 g in 500 ml

b) Cross multiply: 4 g → 100 ml, x → 500 ml
x = 4 × 500 / 100 = 20 g in 500 ml
OR use the formula: Percentage / 100 × Number = 4 / 100 × 500 = 20 g

w/v % = grams in every 100 ml.

8. Heparin & Insulin (Units)

1 lecture questions (total 1)

45
Lecture Question · Slide 18
Heparin is available as 1000, 5000, 10000 and 25000 units/ml (1 ml ampules).
You need to give heparin as continuous infusion 13000 units in 35 ml normal saline over 24 hours. What do you withdraw?

1 ml of 10000 units/ml + 0.6 ml of 5000 units/ml

Total dose: 13000 units
You will need: 1 × 10000 units/ml (1 ml ampule) plus 1 × 5000 units/ml (1 ml ampule)
You need to withdraw 3000 units from the 5000 units ampule:
3000 / 5000 × 1 = 0.6 ml
So, you take 1 ml of the 10000 units/ml ampule + 0.6 ml of the 5000 units/ml ampule

Use the biggest whole ampule first, then take the rest from a smaller one. (Insulin has no calculation — it is calibrated in pens of 100 units/ml.)

Lecture Question · Slide 20
Practice Calculation 1
How many grams of sodium chloride are there in one litre infusion of sodium chloride 0.9%?

9 g

0.9% = 0.9 g in 100 ml
1 litre = 1000 ml
0.9 g → 100 ml, x → 1000 ml
x = 0.9 × 1000 / 100 = 9 g

1000 ml is 10 × 100 ml, so 10 × 0.9 g.

47
Lecture Question · Slide 21
Practice Calculation 2
You need to give infusion of calcium gluconate 3 g. You have 10 ml ampules of calcium gluconate 10%. How much do you need to withdraw?

30 ml (3 ampules)

10% = 10 g in 100 ml = 1 g in 10 ml (one ampule = 1 g)
1 g → 10 ml, 3 g → x
x = 3 × 10 / 1 = 30 ml
So withdraw 30 ml = 3 × 10 ml ampules

Amount you want / amount you have × volume = 3/10 × 100 = 30 ml.
