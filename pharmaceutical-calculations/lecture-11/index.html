1. Isotonic, Hypotonic & Hypertonic

2 lecture (total 2)

1
Lecture Question · Slide 2
Define each type of solution and its effect on red blood cells (RBCs).
a. Isotonic solution

Same osmotic pressure as a body fluid

Ophthalmic, nasal and parenteral solutions should be isotonic.

Iso = same.

b. Hypotonic solution

Lower osmotic pressure than a body fluid → RBCs swell and burst (hemolysis)

Slide 2: “These solutions lead to swelling and bursting of RBCs, which in turn leads to hemolysis.”

Hypo = lower → water moves into the cell → it swells and bursts.

c. Hypertonic solution

Higher osmotic pressure than a body fluid → RBCs shrink

Slide 2: “These solutions lead to shrinkage of the RBCs.”

Hyper = higher → water moves out of the cell → it shrinks.

2. Freezing Point Depression Method

3 lecture · 6 textbook (total 9)

3
Lecture Question · Slide 5
Freezing point depression — the basics.
a. ΔTF = ?

ΔTF = TF (pure solvent) − TF (solution)

Slide 5: the freezing point depression is defined as TF (pure solvent) − TF (solution).

Water freezes at 0 °C, so ΔTF = how far below 0 the solution freezes.

b. ΔTF of blood plasma and tears = ?

0.52 °C

Slide 5: isotonic solutions freeze at −0.52 °C.

Target for every isotonic solution = 0.52 °C.

c. What do hypotonic solutions need?

Addition of a solute (usually NaCl) to depress the freezing point to −0.52 °C

Slide 5.

Add NaCl until ΔTF reaches 0.52 °C.

4
Lecture Question · Slide 6
How many milligrams each of sodium chloride and procaine HCl (fz. pt = −0.122 °C for a 1% solution) are required to prepare 30 mL of a 2% solution of procaine HCl? (ΔTF of 1% NaCl = 0.58 °C)
a. Step 1: ΔTF of the drug = ?

0.244 °C

1% → 0.122 °C
2% → 2 × 0.122 = 0.244 °C

ΔTF is proportional to the % of the drug.

b. Step 2: how much more must the freezing point be lowered?

0.276 °C

0.52 − 0.244 = 0.276 °C

Target 0.52 minus what the drug already gives.

c. Step 3: % of NaCl required = ?

0.476%

1% NaCl → 0.58 °C
x% → 0.276 °C
x = 0.276 / 0.58 = 0.476%

Divide the remaining ΔTF by 0.58 (ΔTF of 1% NaCl, from the table).

d. Step 4: NaCl for 30 mL = ? mg

143 mg

0.476 g in 100 mL
0.476 × 30 / 100 = 0.1428 g ≈ 143 mg

% w/v = g per 100 mL, then scale to 30 mL.

e. Procaine HCl for 30 mL = ? mg

600 mg

2% = 2 g in 100 mL
2 × 30 / 100 = 0.6 g = 600 mg

Answer: 600 mg procaine HCl + 143 mg NaCl.

Lecture Question · Slides 7–8
How many milligrams each of sodium chloride and dibucaine hydrochloride are required to prepare 30 mL of a 1% solution of dibucaine hydrochloride isotonic with tears? (Use the table on slide 8: dibucaine HCl ΔTF 1% = 0.08 °C; sodium chloride = 0.58 °C)
a. NaCl = ? mg

228 mg

Step 1: 1% dibucaine → 0.08 °C
Step 2: 0.52 − 0.08 = 0.44 °C
Step 3: 0.44 / 0.58 = 0.7586% NaCl
Step 4: 0.7586 × 30 / 100 = 0.2276 g ≈ 228 mg

Same 4 steps as procaine; take ΔTF values from the table.

b. Dibucaine HCl = ? mg

300 mg

1% = 1 g in 100 mL
1 × 30 / 100 = 0.3 g = 300 mg

Answer: 300 mg dibucaine HCl + 228 mg NaCl.

Textbook Question · Example 11.10 · Page 207
Calculate the amount of sodium chloride that should be added to the following formulation of nasal drops in order to make the final solution isotonic.
Ephedrine hydrochloride 0.5 g
Water to 100 mL
(A 1% w/v solution of ephedrine hydrochloride depresses the freezing point by 0.169 °C. A 1% w/v solution of sodium chloride depresses the freezing point by 0.576 °C.)
NaCl = ? % w/v

0.756% w/v (0.756 g in 100 mL)

0.5% ephedrine: 0.5 × 0.169 = 0.0845 °C
0.52 − 0.0845 = 0.4355 °C
0.4355 / 0.576 = 0.756% w/v

The textbook uses 0.576 for NaCl; the lecture table rounds it to 0.58.

Textbook Question · Q12 · Page 210
An IV solution of sodium chloride 0.2% needs to be made isotonic by the addition of anhydrous glucose. What weight of anhydrous glucose is required in the preparation of 1 L of the IV solution? (A 1% solution of sodium chloride depresses the freezing point of water by 0.576 °C. A 1% solution of anhydrous glucose depresses the freezing point of water by 0.1037 °C.)
Anhydrous glucose = ? g

39.03 g

0.2% NaCl: 0.2 × 0.576 = 0.1152 °C
0.52 − 0.1152 = 0.4048 °C
Glucose: 0.4048 / 0.1037 = 3.903% w/v
3.903 g per 100 mL × 10 = 39.03 g in 1 L

Here the adjusting agent is glucose, so divide by glucose’s ΔTF (0.1037).

Textbook Question · Q15 · Page 211
Using the information in the previous question (textbook Q14), explain why it may not be possible or necessary to make pilocarpine hydrochloride 4% eye drops isotonic.
Explain

A 4% solution already depresses the freezing point by about 0.524 °C

2% → 0.262 °C
4% → 2 × 0.262 = 0.524 °C ≈ 0.52 °C

It is already about isotonic, so no NaCl needs to be added.

3. NaCl Equivalents (E-value)

2 lecture (total 2)

12
Lecture Question · Slide 9
The procedure for calculating isotonic solutions with NaCl.
a. Step 1

Assume the solution contains only NaCl and calculate the grams of NaCl needed to make it isotonic

Isotonic NaCl = 0.9% → volume (mL) × 0.009 = g NaCl

0.9% NaCl is isotonic.

b. Step 2

Grams of NaCl represented by each ingredient = weight of drug × E-value

Slide 9: “weight of drug × e-value = equivalent weight of NaCl.”

E-value = how many g of NaCl 1 g of the drug is worth.

c. Step 3

Step 1 − Step 2 = grams of NaCl to be added

Slide 9: subtract the NaCl represented by the ingredients from the NaCl alone.

What is still missing = what you add.

Lecture Question · Slide 10
How many grams of sodium chloride should be used in compounding the following prescription?
Rx Pilocarpine nitrate 0.3 g
Sodium chloride q.s.
Purified water ad 30 mL
Make isoton. sol.
Sig. For the eye
(E-value of pilocarpine nitrate = 0.23)
NaCl = ? g

0.201 g

Step 1: 30 × 0.009 = 0.27 g NaCl
Step 2: 0.3 × 0.23 = 0.069 g
Step 3: 0.27 − 0.069 = 0.201 g

The E-value is not on the slide; 0.23 is the standard table value (Ansel).

4. Using an Agent Other Than NaCl

3 lecture (total 3)

14
Lecture Question · Slide 11
If an agent other than NaCl (e.g. boric acid, dextrose, potassium nitrate) is used to make the solution isotonic, what is Step 4?
Step 4

Divide the grams of NaCl (Step 3) by the E-value of the other substance

Slide 11: “divide the amount of NaCl (Step 3) by the sodium chloride equivalent of the other substance.”

g of agent = g NaCl ÷ E of the agent.

15
Lecture Question · Slide 12
How many grams of boric acid should be used in compounding the following prescription?
Rx Phenacaine hydrochloride 1%
Chlorobutanol ½%
Boric acid q.s.
Purified water ad 60 mL
Make isoton. sol.
Sig. One drop in each eye
(E-values: phenacaine HCl = 0.20 · chlorobutanol = 0.24 · boric acid = 0.52)
a. Step 1: NaCl alone = ? g

0.54 g

60 × 0.009 = 0.54 g

0.9% of 60 mL.

b. Step 2: NaCl represented by the ingredients = ? g

0.192 g

Phenacaine: 1% of 60 mL = 0.6 g × 0.20 = 0.12 g
Chlorobutanol: ½% of 60 mL = 0.3 g × 0.24 = 0.072 g
Total = 0.12 + 0.072 = 0.192 g

Convert % to grams first, then × E-value.

c. Step 3: NaCl still needed = ? g

0.348 g

0.54 − 0.192 = 0.348 g

Step 1 − Step 2.

d. Step 4: boric acid = ? g

0.67 g

0.348 / 0.52 = 0.669 g ≈ 0.67 g

Divide by the E-value of boric acid.
