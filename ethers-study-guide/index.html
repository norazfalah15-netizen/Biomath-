Question 1
Define ethers and draw the general structure.
Show answer
An ether has an oxygen atom bonded to two carbon groups (alkyl or aryl).
R–O–R  ·  R–O–R′  ·  Ar–O–R  ·  Ar–O–Ar
Question 2
Provide a summary of the general rules to name ethers (common & IUPAC).
Show answer
Common nomenclature
The alkyl groups connected to oxygen are named first in alphabetical order, followed by the word “ether”.

IUPAC nomenclature
  1. Find the longest continuous chain of carbon atoms.
  2. Identify the substituents attached to the chain (alkyl groups containing oxygen = alkoxy).
  3. Number the chain and list the substituents in alphabetical order.
Note: Alcohol (OH) gets the priority over ether (C–O–C) in naming and numbering.

Question 5
What can you conclude from the following table? Compare ethers with alkanes and alcohols in terms of solubility and bp. Justify your answer.
Show answer
Alcohols have a higher bp than ethers, due to H-bonding.
Ethers can accept H-bonds but cannot donate them. This makes them soluble in water, but there is no H-bonding between ether molecules, so they have bps similar to alkanes. Alcohols can H-bond to each other (like water), so they have significantly higher bps.

Question 8
Use the structure of an ether to indicate the reaction sites. How many reaction sites are there? (list them)
Show answer
Two reaction sites:
  1. Two polar bonds (C–O): C is δ⁺, O is δ⁻
  2. Two non-bonding electron pairs on O
Question 9
Write the general reaction of an ether with ONE and TWO moles of hydrogen halide (HX). Also indicate by which mechanism the reactions occur.
Show answer
Nucleophilic substitution reaction (SN1 and SN2 mechanisms)
R–O–R + HX  →  R–X + R–OH   (1 mole)
R–O–R + 2 HX  →  2 R–X + H₂O   (2 moles)
X = Br or I (heat). With 2 moles, the alcohol formed reacts with the second HX to give a second alkyl halide.
SN1Cleavage at a 2° or 3° carbon of the ether; 2-step process (via a carbocation)
SN2Cleavage at a 1° carbon (or CH₃) of the ether; 1-step process
